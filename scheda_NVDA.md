# NVIDIA Corp — Credit Assessment
## Five-year Senior Unsecured Facility

---

### 1. Issuer and basis

**Issuer:** NVIDIA Corp (CIK 0001045810). **Period:** fiscal year 2024-02-01 to 2025-01-31. **Source:** annual report accession 0001045810-25-000023, US GAAP, USD. **Information cutoff:** 2025-06-01. **Perimeter:** consolidated; `perimeter_status` is `unavailable`, so the base cannot show which entity holds the cash or where the debt sits. The consolidated figures are treated as a group-level upper bound on what a creditor of the borrowing entity can rely on; that boundary is assumed, not established.

---

### 2. Assessment

The issuer is at essentially zero leverage against enormous, cash-backed earnings generation; the binding constraint is not solvency but the size of the facility relative to a genuinely tiny debt stack and a razor-fine maturity profile that the filing does not disclose. A five-year unsecured facility is comfortably serviceable on any earnings scenario this filing supports, with the caveat that the financing gap between the group's balance sheet and the specific borrowing entity cannot be shown from the consolidated base.

---

### 3. Key drivers

**Leverage is trivially low in absolute terms, but cannot be stated as a ratio.**
Reported financial debt is $8.463bn (verified, summing the debt components). House adjusted debt is $10.27bn (lower_bound, `reported` debt plus both operating lease components), and it is a floor not a figure because no finance lease total could be established — a missing addend can only raise it. House operating EBITDA is $83.317bn (derived, `reported` operating income + `reported` operating D&A). Even on the floor, adjusted debt is roughly one-eighth of a year's EBITDA. The ratios themselves cannot be printed: a lower_bound in the numerator and a derived denominator mean any leverage number would inherit the weakest state and claim more than it shows. Even a tenfold error either way leaves the position unthreatened. No sector threshold is available, so take this as my own level, not the sector's: leverage would have to approach roughly 2x on house operating EBITDA before a five-year unsecured facility became a question of capacity rather than convenience.

**Cash generation dwarfs every cash obligation and, unusually, is verified at the cash line.**
CFO of $64.089bn (reported) exceeds reported financial debt nearly eight times. The bridge from EBITDA of $83.317bn to CFO widens by $18.7bn from combined interest (cash interest $0.246bn), cash taxes ($15.118bn) and a working-capital outflow of $13.063bn (reported as the accounts-receivable movement alone). That working-capital draw is the one thing in the bridge that could be a problem; the note flags it as the receivables line and nothing else, so persistence cannot be tested from a single period.

**Free operating cash flow alone services debt without any refinancing and without touching equity.**
FOCF is $60.853bn (derived, CFO less capex of $3.236bn reported). DCF after the substantial $34.54bn of dividends plus buybacks is still $26.313bn, which alone is three times the entire debt stack. The discretionary-flow figure is the conservative one to present to a senior lender, because it answers what is retained after NVIDIA's committed shareholder return policy.

---

### 4. Financial profile

- **House operating EBITDA:** $83.317bn — derived, `reported` operating income plus `reported` operating D&A. Names a house convention; reproduces no agency's adjustments, so no agency threshold applies.
- **House FFO:** $67.953bn — derived, EBITDA less cash interest and cash tax, before working capital. Not an agency FFO.
- **FOCF:** $60.853bn — derived, CFO ($64.089bn, reported) less capex ($3.236bn, reported).
- **DCF:** $26.313bn — derived, FOCF less dividends ($0.834bn) and repurchases ($33.706bn), both reported.
- **House adjusted debt:** ≥$10.27bn — lower_bound, `reported` debt ($8.463bn) plus operating leases ($1.807bn, derived). Finance leases unavailable and can only raise the floor.
- **Net debt:** unavailable. House adjusted debt is not usable, so nothing built on it — including netting cash of $8.589bn against it — can stand.

**Coverage is effectively not a constraint.** Charged interest ($0.247bn, reported) and cash interest ($0.246bn, reported) are nearly identical, so there is no capitalised/lease-interest wedge inflating the P&L cost. House FFO covers cash interest ~276x on the derived figures. Coverage answers affordability; it says nothing about repayment of principal, which at this scale is the non-issue.

**Earnings quality caveats.** Cash taxes paid of $15.118bn against operating income of $81.453bn is a genuinely heavy cash-tax load already embedded in CFO, which makes the cash generation the more credible. Working-capital movement is disclosed only as the receivables line and consumed $13.06bn; with a single period the persistence separately of receivables growth versus payables/inventory is untested. `impairment`, `lease_fleet_investment` and disposals are all unavailable; none is likely material at this profile, but a one-period recurrence of any of them cannot be judged.

---

### 5. Liquidity and debt structure

- **Cash actually available:** $8.589bn (reported, cash and equivalents). No restricted cash is disclosed; no restriction is subtracted. This is consolidated cash and does not establish which legal entity holds it or whether it can reach the borrowing entity's debt.
- **Committed facility:** $0.575bn maximum borrowing capacity (reported). This is the full committed line; undrawn availability is not disclosed. For a facility of the size a five-year senior unsecured lender to NVIDIA would consider, a $575m committed line is material to *itself* only in a stress where all other sources have closed, and it is four orders of magnitude below the group's DCF.
- **Maturity ladder: unknown.** Current maturities of long-term debt are reported at zero and short-term borrowings at zero, so nothing falls due inside twelve months on the reported figures. But the distribution of the $8.463bn of non-current debt by year is not in the base. For a five-year facility this is the single most material gap: it determines whether the group carries a refi wall inside the facility's own tenor.
- **What the group is relying on:** nothing. There is no refinancing, disposal or recovery assumption anywhere in this profile. Operating cash flow alone services the entire debt stack annually with no capital-market access.

---

### 6. Sensitivities

- **Upside / neutral under any plausible scenario.** Even a 50% collapse in house FFO (to ~$34bn) still covers the entire adjusted-debt floor three times over in a single year's operations. There is no earnings scenario this filing supports that makes the facility's interest unserviceable.
- **What would change the view — my own levels, not the sector's:**
  - **Cash cycle deterioration:** a working-capital outflow at the current $13bn pace persisting three consecutive periods while CFO simultaneously declines — that combination, not either alone, would signal growth funding itself into a liquidity drain rather than into cash.
  - **Shareholder distributions were the drawdown.** DCF is the honest figure precisely because NVIDIA returns $34.5bn a year to shareholders. If the market turns and CFO falls toward the midpoint while the $34.5bn distribution is maintained, DCF could approach the residual-creditor break-even within two periods. The trigger is distribution maintained *against* a CFO decline, not the decline alone.
  - **Leverage:** should adjusted debt approach ~2x house operating EBITDA (my level), a five-year unsecured facility changes from convenience to structural question.
- **Downside mechanism:** not an inability to pay — at any lever this filing supports the facility is repaid from operations. The downside is covenant/documented unsecured posture: on this base an unsecured lender has claim to the same asset pool as all other senior creditors against a realistically tiny debt stock, so the real risk is security, priority and the undemonstrable perimeter, not credit loss.

---

### 7. Assumptions

- **Perimeter assumed, not established.** The $8.589bn consolidated cash, the $8.463bn debt and the CFO are group-level; the borrowing entity's own cash, its guarantee coverage of group obligations, and where the debt sits against the repaying assets are all unshown. Every group-level figure is treated as an upper bound on the borrower's position.
- **House adjusted debt assumes the lease convention of capitalising both operating lease components** onto reported debt, consistent with the two major agencies that capitalise. Because finance leases are missing the figure is a floor by construction.
- **All loan components are assumed to be at the group** where the leverage and coverage are computed; no guarantee structure is disclosed to confirm this.
- **Accounts-receivable growth is treated as a genuine operating draw** (the conservative reading) rather than as a collections or channel indicator; a single period cannot distinguish them.

---

### 8. What could not be established

- **The maturity ladder of the $8.463bn of non-current debt.** This, more than anything, decides the question for a five-year unsecured lender — whether the group carries a refinancing concentration inside the facility's tenor. The statements show zero current maturities but no year-by-year distribution; a single reported table (the Schedule of Debt with years of maturity) would resolve it, and it is the missing piece most likely to change the practical answer.
- **Finance lease liabilities (total, current, non-current).** Unavailable; their absence makes adjusted debt a floor, not a figure. The filing's debt note is the only thing that settles whether any already sit inside reported debt.
- **The perimeter gap.** `perimeter_status` is unavailable, so structural subordination and cash location — the two things that separate a strong group from a safe lender — are unquantified.
- **Undrawn committed availability** beyond the $575m maximum capacity; uncommitted lines are not a liquidity source and none are disclosed.

The overall view rests on one irreducible fact: the group generates $60bn+ of free operating cash flow a year against a debt stack that, even at its floor and then adjusted tenfold for every unknown in the perimeter, is a fraction of a single year's operations. That fact is well supported by the reported cash line and is hard to disturb. The lender's diligence should concentrate not on credit capacity — which is not credibly in question — but on the maturity ladder and the perimeter, for which this filing does not provide the answer.
