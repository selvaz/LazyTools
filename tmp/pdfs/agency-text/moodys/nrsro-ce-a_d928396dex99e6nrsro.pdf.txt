Exhibit 6 
 
Conflicts of Interest Related to the 
Issuance of Credit Ratings 
 
MIS has identified the following types of conflicts of interest related to the issuance of 
credit ratings: 
1. MIS is paid by issuers or underwriters to determine credit ratings with respect to 
securities or money market instruments they issue or underwrite. 
2. MIS is paid by obligors to determine credit ratings of the obligors. 
3. MIS is paid by investors to determine credit ratings with respect to securities or 
money market instruments. 
4. In addition to credit ratings, MIS provides other services to issuers, underwriters or 
obligors that may be subject to a credit rating by MIS. MIS is paid for these other services 
by the requesting issuer or obligor. 
5. MIS issues and maintains a credit rating for a security or money market instrument 
issued by an asset pool or as a part of an asset-backed or mortgage-backed securities 
transaction that are paid for by the issuer, sponsor, or underwriter of the security or 
money market instruments. 
6. In addition to credit ratings, MIS analytic staff may spend time producing research 
publications. 
7. MIS analytic staff may engage in dialogue with and receive feedback from market 
participants, including issuers, underwriters, obligors, investors, intermediaries, and 
others. Those market participants may have an economic interest in the issuers, 
securities, or money market instruments for which MIS determines credit ratings. 
8. MIS allows persons within MIS to directly own securities or money market instruments 
of, or have other direct ownership interests in, obligors or issuers subject to a credit 
rating determined by MIS as long as they do not participate in or otherwise influence the 
credit rating for such obligors or issuers. 
9. MIS allows persons within MIS to have business relationships that are more than 
arm’s length ordinary course business relationships with obligors or issuers subject to a 
credit rating determined by MIS as long as they do not participate in or otherwise 
influence the credit rating for such obligors or issuers. 
10. MIS and/or its affiliates are paid by persons for subscriptions to receive or access

MIS’s credit ratings and/or for other services offered by MIS where such person (i) 
may use the credit ratings to comply with, and obtain benefits or relief under, statutes 
using the term nationally recognized statistical rating organization, and/or (ii) also 
may own investments or have entered into transactions that could be favorably or 
adversely impacted by a credit rating issued by MIS. 
11. Members of the Boards of Directors of MIS or its Credit Rating Affiliates may be affiliated 
with obligors or issuers subject to a credit rating determined by MIS. 
12. Members of the Board of Directors of Moody’s Corporation (the parent of MIS) 
may be affiliated with obligors or issuers subject to a credit rating determined by 
MIS. 
13. MIS may issue credit ratings on, and/or requested by, entities which may have 
significant shareholdings (5% or more of outstanding shares) in Moody's 
Corporation. 
14. MIS may employ staff who were previously employed by obligors or issuers subject 
to a credit rating determined by MIS.  
15. MIS may provide credit rating services to customers of its affiliates. 
16. MIS may issue credit ratings on transactions where one of its affiliates is a party or 
otherwise provides a service or product to the obligor or issuer, such that MIS’s 
methodologies require MIS to assess the service or product being provided by the 
affiliate. 
17. Moody’s Corporation, and/or its affiliates, may engage underwriters or investment bankers 
in connection with acquisitions, joint ventures and/or the sale by Moody’s Corporation of 
debt or other securities. Moody’s Corporation also has commercial relationships with 
numerous corporations from which it purchases goods or services (e.g., utilities, travel, 
technology hardware or software, office supplies, etc.) or which hold ownership interests in 
businesses in which Moody’s Corporation also has an ownership interest. The parties with 
which Moody’s Corporation has such business relationships may be entities about which, 
or to which, MIS provides credit ratings.
