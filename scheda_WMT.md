## 2. Assessment

The issuer carries low absolute leverage and a maturity profile almost entirely beyond the facility horizon, with the earnings and cash flow to service its fixed charges comfortably on the evidence available. The credit is strong on what the base can show; the base cannot show all of what the retail sector requires.

## 3. Key drivers

**Leverage is low on reported earnings but is understated, and by an unquantified amount.** Adjusted debt (derived, house convention: leases capitalised, both lease liabilities added to the three debt components) of 60.1bn against house operating EBITDA (derived, operating income 29.35bn plus operating D&A 12.97bn, both `reported`) of 42.3bn gives about **1.4x**. House FFO (derived off the same inputs) gives **1.8x**. Both are reported-quality conclusions. Because this is a US GAAP retailer, operating rent sits inside the operating income used to build EBITDA; adding lease debt to that denominator compares a lease-inclusive debt with a gross-of-rent earnings, so the 1.4x understates true leverage. The base contains no rent or EBITDAR figure with which to correct it, so the understatement is real but not quantified.

**Interest coverage is ample, but it is not fixed-charge coverage.** Operating income (reported) of 29.35bn covers cash interest paid (reported) of 2.74bn about **10.7x**. Coverage on cash interest answers whether the money left; it does not count rent, which in retail is a fixed charge with the same failure consequence as interest. True fixed-charge coverage is lower and is not computable from this base.

**Cash generation is substantial before distributions and thin after them.** Free operating cash flow (derived, CFO 36.44bn less house capex 23.78bn) of 12.66bn comfortably exceeds the next five years' total principal maturities (about 13.1bn), but discretionary cash flow after dividends (6.69bn) and buybacks (4.49bn) of 1.48bn is thin against the 60.1bn debt stock. Debt is not being retired from retained cash in a meaningful way; it is being serviced and refinanced.

**The maturity profile is the strongest single feature.** Of 60.1bn adjusted debt, principal schedule shows only 2.60bn falling in year one, 3.45bn, 1.74bn, 3.34bn and 1.96bn in years two through five, and 22.9bn beyond — all reported. There is no maturity wall within a five-year senior unsecured facility horizon.

- **The direction working-capital takes.** A continued outflow in the 1.11bn range would be funding growth; a swing to a large inflow would be inventory sell-down, with markdown consequences in retail.
